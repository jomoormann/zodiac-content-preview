Draft your first setup in minutes with constellations

September 22, 2026 · Jan-Felix Schwarz · 6 min read

The simulator in Part 1 let you click an organization together: policies, members, a timelock, a council. This part gets you the same thing for your own organization, as something you can hold: a constellation, your setup written as code.

A constellation is a small repository. It declares your safes, your policies, who holds them, and the limits on everything. push compares it with the chain and opens the difference for review in the Zodiac App. Nothing deploys, nothing changes, until your owners sign. That property is what makes the rest of this post safe to do: drafting is free.

To be clear about what you will have at the end: a showcase. Placeholder addresses, unreviewed numbers, nothing onchain. That is the point. Drafting your structure is now the cheap part; making it real is the part worth doing carefully, and there is an offer for that at the end.

The guided path

  1. Start from a worked example. We published the simulator's own organization as a repo: zodiac-sim-org-constellation. Three safes, one Roles modifier, three policies, and a README that explains every wiring decision, including the timelock. Fork it, or start from the blank template.
  2. Write your requirements like you would brief a colleague. Who acts. Which routine actions they need. Which limits feel right. Who governs, and how fast. Plain sentences are enough; the prompt below shows the shape.
  3. Point an AI assistant at the docs. The developer docs ship an llms.txt exactly for this: your assistant reads the whole reference in one go. Then give it the prompt below with your requirements filled in.
  4. Read what it wrote. The layout maps one-to-one to your brief: roles/<name>/members.ts is who, permissions.ts is what. The permissions read like sentences, and that is deliberate: 
    allow.eth.usdc.approve(POOL),
allow.eth.aave_v3.pool.supply(USDC, c.withinAllowance(weekly.key), c.avatar),
allow.eth.aave_v3.pool.withdraw(USDC, undefined, c.avatar),
    Approve to one pool, supply within the weekly budget for the vault, withdraw only back to the vault. If a line does not read like something you meant, that is your review finding.
  5. Push, and look at the diff. bun push opens your draft as a reviewable change set in the Zodiac App. Still nothing onchain. It becomes real only when real owners sign, and this draft has none.

This workflow is not hypothetical: our public demo organization was drafted exactly this way, by an AI, across seven DeFi protocols. It is a showcase, not a deployment: the AI wrote every file and could not move a cent, because writing files is not authority. Signing is.

The prompt

Adapt the bracketed parts. The rules section is not decoration: it encodes the sharp edges we hit building the example repos, so leave it in.

Prompt · copy and adapt the [brackets] 
You are helping me draft a Zodiac constellation: my organization's onchain
setup as code. This is a showcase draft, not a production deployment.

Read first:
- Docs: https://docs.zodiac.eco/llms-full.txt
- Template: github.com/gnosisguild/zodiac-constellation-template
- Worked example: github.com/jomoormann/zodiac-sim-org-constellation

My organization:
- People: [Ana (operations), Ben (trading), Mia (payroll)] and [one automation key]
- Treasury holds [ETH and USDC] on [Ethereum mainnet]
- Routine actions to delegate: [deposit idle USDC into Aave v3, up to
  100k per week] · [pay 3 team wallets, up to 30k per month] ·
  [swap ETH/USDC on CoW]
- Governance: [2/3] admin safe acting through a [24h] timelock,
  security council [4/7] as the treasury's owner (veto + exit)

Rules:
- Roles v2 only: no membership expiry, no USD-priced budgets, no slippage
  bounds. Say so in comments where v3 would express something better.
- Placeholder addresses only. Never ask for keys. Never deploy anything.
- Pin every withdrawal and receiver parameter to the vault where possible.
- Prefer DeFi Kit presets; where a preset cannot meter amounts, hand-write
  the permission with c.withinAllowance instead.
- Output the constellation/ files, plus a table of who can do what, with
  limits.

From showcase to production

A draft like this is deliberately not deployable. Between a showcase and a production setup sit the questions that actually matter: the real signer sets, allowance sizes that fit your flows, the timelock wiring details (the example repo's README documents the ones that bit us, down to why the Delay's owner must never be the operator), monitoring, and recovery.
