The five deep dives it teases

Each aspect gets its own post. Part 5 is a proposed addition to the original four; it targets the agent-builder audience the site already speaks to, and is easy to cut if the series should stay at four.

PART 2

Routine actions on a fast lane

Claims, rebalances, and payouts run in one transaction instead of a signing round: record a permission by demonstrating it, simulate it on a fork, batch the workflow. With real recorded flows from Aave and Morpho.

PART 3

Controls anyone can verify, a vendor nobody needs

The policy engine is contract state on your account, not a database on our servers. We read a live setup's permissions off a block explorer, then ask the exit question: what still works if Zodiac disappears?

PART 4

Blast radius is a design decision

Roles scope to contracts, functions, parameters, amounts, and destinations. What a leaked key can reach on Saturday is exactly what its role allowed on Friday: nothing else.

PART 5

Your organization as code

Vault, roles, members, and caps in a git repository: forked, reviewed like a pull request, signed by the account. Including what happened when an AI wrote our entire setup and still could not move a cent.

PART 6

Agents under mandate

An agent will eventually try something outside its mandate. With budgets, expiries, and boundaries enforced by the account rather than by the agent's good behavior, trying is all it can do.

Using the visual

  • In the post: embed the SVG inline (file: zodiac-overview-visual.svg) so it inherits the site's real Schibsted Grotesk and JetBrains Mono; as a standalone file it falls back to system fonts.
  • On X and LinkedIn: export a 2x PNG of the full panel for the opener thread. The account group alone (Roles gate + vault + revert bounce) crops into a strong standalone card for the short-form drip.
  • It scales down honestly: at timeline size the three zone labels, the two lanes, and the bounce still read; the policy pills become texture, which is fine.
  • Accuracy notes for review: policy examples are modeled on the demo org's real caps; "2/3" quorum and the Friday mandate are illustrative. Confirm the tool naming (App · Pilot · Constellations) matches how the team wants the product surface described.
