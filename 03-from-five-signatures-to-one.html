From five signatures to one, without giving up control

September 22, 2026 · Jan-Felix Schwarz · 7 min read

A routine treasury action carries a hidden price: the ceremony. Propose it, ping the second signer, wait out a time zone, chase the third, execute a day later into a price that has moved. The action was never the hard part. The coordination was.

On Zodiac, routine work runs differently: one team member, one signature, executed immediately, inside limits the account enforces on every single call. The full quorum still exists, but it signs the rules, not every use of them. If you played the simulator in Part 1, you built exactly this split.

This part is about making that one signature not just safe, but genuinely fast. It is the part where you meet the browser extension.

One signature, inside hard limits

Take Ana's Deposits policy from the simulator: $100,000 a week, into approved protocols only, everything returning to the vault. Inside those limits she moves at the speed of any hot wallet. Outside them, every transaction reverts before it can matter. The quorum is for changing the rules; the fast lane is for using them.

The extension

Zodiac Pilot sits in your browser and connects to your organization's vault like a wallet. You open the interfaces your team already uses, Aave, Uniswap, Lido, whatever the workflow touches, and do the work the way you always do. Pilot handles everything between your click and the chain:

  • Record. Nothing is sent onchain while you work. Pilot captures the exact contract calls each interface produces.
  • Batch, across frontends. Claim rewards on one app, swap them on a second, deposit the result on a third. Pilot collects every step into a single atomic transaction: if any call fails, all of it reverts. No stranded funds halfway through a workflow.
  • Simulate. Before anything is real, the batch runs against a fork of current chain state. You see which tokens move, how balances change, and whether your policy allows every call. Surprises happen in the fork, not in the treasury.
  • Find the route. When more than one of your roles could carry a call, Pilot checks the permission paths and picks one that clears. You do not think about which role you are wearing.
  • Sign once. One signature executes the whole batch through the policy check. And when a recording contains something your role does not allow yet, one click turns it into a permission request for the owners to sign: the recording is the specification.

    What this changes for a team

    • Time. Multi-step workflows execute in one go instead of five signing rounds, and permission setup drops from an engineering task to a screen recording.
    • Certainty. Simulation shows the exact outcome and the policy check runs before execution. A revert costs a simulation, not gas and an awkward message in the group chat.
    • Exactness without the pain. Permissions match what your team actually does, because they are derived from what your team actually did.
    • The guardrails stay on. Everything Pilot executes still passes through the Roles check onchain. Recording makes permissions easy to create, not easy to escape.

    Pilot runs in Chrome and Chromium browsers, works with any dapp interface, and needs no integration from the dapp's side.
